01  THE PROBLEM

The alarm is easy. The answer is not.

Crew Control does not struggle to notice that something broke. They struggle to work out what breaks next, and who can legally be moved to cover it — across a roster, seven duty clocks, licence expiries, aircraft ratings, base rules and a standby list.

And it is arithmetic, not judgement. A pilot may work 60 hours in any 7 days. Covering this trip puts Captain C-2087 on 61 hours 20 minutes. That is 2% over. In aviation 2% over is not "close enough" — it is illegal.

A nearly-right answer is not a slightly worse answer. It is a violation.
02  THE ARCHITECTURE

The model does the talking. Code does the deciding.

LayerWhat it doesWhat it may never do
Language modelTurn a sentence into a typed query. Write the answer back in plain English.Compute. Rank. Decide legality.
KernelEvery number. All seven rules. Cost, ranking, legality.Guess. It has no fallback.
GuardReads the finished sentence back against the ledger.Let an unbacked figure reach the screen.

The model never sees a raw duty clock. It only ever sees figures the kernel already produced, so it cannot get the arithmetic wrong — it never sees the arithmetic. Delete the model entirely and the system still answers 42 of 42 correctly.

07  CASE FIVE · REFUSAL

Four ways to say no, and they are not the same no

AskedAnswerWhy it is a different refusal
"What is C-3310's phone number?"Not heldWe hold rosters, not contact details. We list what we do hold.
"Will C-2087 call in sick tomorrow?"Not oursThat is a prediction. The risk score was handed to us; we say where it came from.
"Who is on reserve on 2026-10-05?"Past horizonThe roster stops on 20 Sep. Beyond it we cannot verify, so we do not claim.
"Tell me about C-9999"No such crewWe never resolve to the nearest match. An invented crew member is a failure, not a rounding error.

Each refusal names what would make it answerable. "I don't hold that" and "I didn't understand you" are different problems, and a tool that hides confusion behind polite humility gets caught the first time you rephrase.

08  CASE SIX · THE CALLOUT

The message goes out in the language they read

A callout is the one thing on this desk that leaves the building, and an Indian carrier's cabin crew do not all read English equally well. Ten languages, via Sarvam.

Drafted after authorisation · Hindi नमस्कार कैप्टन डी. रेड्डी, यह क्रू कंट्रोल है।
DUTY: P-2291 on 2026-09-15
REPORT: 06:00 UTC at BLR
SECTORS: DX412, DX413, DX588
Translatedthe wording only
Neverthe trip, the station, the sectors or the Zulu time
WhyMeasured. Shielding identifiers with markers lost DX412/413/588 in Hindi, Bengali and Marathi, inconsistently. Two parts made the guarantee structural: 7/7 identifiers intact, up from 5/7.

Why this is safe when nothing else model-written would be

Every figure in a callout is computed by the kernel and passed by the number guard before Sarvam sees it. Translation re-says a verified sentence — it cannot invent a report time. It is also how aviation already works: identifiers stay in standard form on every flight deck, in every language.

11  HONEST LIMITS

Where it stops, and what it says when it gets there

  • One week only. The roster ends 2026-09-20. Rest before a trip on the 21st is unverifiable, so we say "legal within the week we can see" rather than claim a certainty we do not have.
  • Two simultaneous disruptions, handled. Three or more we still return a legal plan for — we just stop calling it the best one.
  • Crew only. No aircraft swaps, no passenger rebooking, no hotels. Real parts of the job, deliberately out of scope.
  • "Passengers" means seats. The data has aircraft capacity, not bookings. 486 is 486 seats, and we say so.
  • Seven rules is the whole scope. Not DGCA CAR Section 7 — seven simplified rules, and the UI says that rather than claiming conformance to a regulation many times larger.

The correction we are keeping visible

An earlier version of this deck claimed the FDP boundary rested on a single witness record. Two independent sweeps found no exact FDP ties in the roster at all. The claim is withdrawn. What replaced it is stronger and verified: of 206 rostered assignments, 200 depart from the crew member's own base — and the six that do not are one trip, P-2289, whose entire crew reports at DEL while based at BLR.
